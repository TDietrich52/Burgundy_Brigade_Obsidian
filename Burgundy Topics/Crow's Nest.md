# History
## The Vision.
* The history of The Crow's Nest and the worship of Wee Jas can be dated back to 2000 years ago (year 4500).
	* It is likely that Wee Jas worship predated this time period, however fossil records are extremely unreliable from this time period, making it extremely difficult to identify how far spread her worship was.
	* Most historians agree that prior to the days of [[Old Burgundy (3669)]], if Wee Jas was worshipped then her cult was highly localized in the [[Grim Mountains]].
* In the year 4395, Wee Jas gave a vision to her most devout of followers, Eileen 'Crows Eye'.
	* Eileen, likely herself a Wicker, is a figure mentioned in surviving documents at the time of Old Burgundy, though she was not in Burgundy during the time of the Mountain King.
	* In the vision, Eileen was shown a black temple on the shore with 12 massive ravens made of blue flame standing guard, with more ravens looking overhead from nearby cliffs.  The location described is nearly identical to the location of the Crow's Nest today.
	* Wee Jas told Eileen that a temple must be built at this site so that her followers might have the strength to carry out her will in the Material Plane.
## Pilgrimage.
* In 4396, Eileen made her pilgramage to the [[Grim Mountains]] in order to spread the faith of Wee Jas and build the temple she saw in her vision.
	* Over the next 4 years, she wandered throughout the mountains and countryside, either gathering the local cults that worshipped Wee Jas or converting people, sources vary.
* In 4402, Eileen and her train of followers, who she dubbed "Crows" arrived at the location seen in Eileen's vision.
	* Over the course of the next century, they constructed the Crow's Nest, codified their beliefs into a series of books (chief among them being Eileen's "On Life and Death"), grew their following from a group in the dozens to a group in the hundreds, and earned recognition across the Rainbow Coast for preventing a plague that would've otherwise devasted Burgundy.
## Founding.
* Two events of note happened in 4500.
	* first Eileen 'Crows Eye', whom her followers had come to know as simply the High Crow, passed away.  She was 900 at the time of her death.  With her passing the first Council of Crows was called and with it, the election of High Crow Lance.
		* Eileen's last act before she passed was to Sanctify the surrounding area, transforming it into Hallowed grounds that only those who who were allies of [[Wee Jas]] could walk upon.
			* This included a nearby group of [[Flora]] worshippers who had recently gathered in the wilderness to reconnect with their goddess.
	* Second, the Crow's Nest officially finished construction.
		* A grand cathedral capable of housing over 15,000 people.

# Beliefs
## Major Tenets
* The Crows believe [[Wee Jas]] is a goddess of Life and Death and that it is her duty to determine when people live and die.
* As such, they have a strong dislike for the dead.
	* There are some exceptions: some members of the Crow's Nest are fine with vampirism so long as the vampires in question do not spread it to others (the extremists want to kill them all and be done with it.)
		* A handful of the more extreme followers of Wee Jas have taken this intense dislike of vampiric and otherworldly forces and morphed it into an excuse to discriminate against other gods and religions under the guise of being a lesser form of denying Wee Jas what she is owed.
	* They believe that every living thing has a pre-determined fate on when they shall perish, and thus it is the height of human foolishness to believe anyone has the power to disturb that order.
* They have made it a habit to collect and control all information relating to the practice of [[Necromancy]].
	* As a goddess of life and death, Wee Jas could be said to have necrotic powers, and thus the study of [[Necromancy]] is not as surprising as one might expect in the Crow's Nest.
		* Disease and plague, especially those related to blight and lichdom, are not seen as natural occurrences in the faith of Wee Jas, but rather unnatural afflictions caused by outside forces (and often undead ones).  Hence why the Crow's Nest tends to be at the front lines whenever a pandemic or blight is under effect.
			*  To this end, it is both official policy and considered good etiquette for members of the Crows Nest to wear masks at all times unless in the company of trusted allies.
				* This is also done to teach followers to reject their egocentrism and view themselves as Wee Jas’s will made manifest, a physical extension of herself into the mortal plane that cannot afford to act on self-interest.
			* Non-Crow followers wear simple face coverings and unadorned masks.
			* Crows are given birdlike plague masks to protect and identify themselves, which is where they got their name.
## Magic
*  Most practioners of [[Wee Jas]] view healing magic as the goddess giving people the strength they need to continue living until their appointed date.
	* Most priests of Wee Jas view their magic as coming directly from their connection to Wee Jas, allowing them to heal the injured, cure disease, or strike down those that oppose them and Wee Jas’s will.
	* When striking down a creature that has opposed them, rather than kill them outright or try and stabilize them, a priest of Wee Jas will instead let the goddess decide their fate.
	* When healing or stabilizing a fallen ally, the priest channels Wee Jas’s power and surrender control, letting it flow through them as Wee Jas sees fit.
		* There is great scholarly debate in the Crow's Nest whether the spell *Revivify* counts as resurrecting the dead.
			* The debate all boils down to whether or not it takes longer than a minute for someone to fully die and for their soul to leave their body.
			* Most Crow's will spend so long internally debating that over a minute will pass and it ultimately won't end up mattering.
## Necromancy
* The Crow's Nest keeps a tight control on all information they can find relating to the undead.
	* They teach their followers at a young age that attempting to artificially extend one's life or revive the dead is blasphemy in the highest order.
	* As a goddess of life and death, Wee Jas is seen as being in charge of maintaining the cycle of creation, so any attempt to resurrect the dead is a perversion of this natural process and a direct insult and challenge to Wee Jas’s divinity.
		-   To the Crows Nest, these methods are seen as robbing Wee Jas of what she is rightfully owed and decreed, and these heretics are punished with a swift end.
- Mortal Sacrifice is strictly forbidden within the Crow's Nest, and even the sacrifice of animals is highly frowned upon and often grounds for ex-communication.
	- Life is sacred in the Crow's Nest and is not to be tossed away carelessly.
- The priests and members of Wee Jas are free to demonstrate their faith to the goddess in whatever method best suits them.
	-   So long as it does not contradict the official doctrine regarding the practices of resurrection and life extension.
## Gender in The Crow's Nest
* There isn't really a sense of gender within the Crow's Nest, even amongst the lowly followers of [[Wee Jas]].
* This is because part of their tenets is to view themselves not as individuals, but as a collective manifestation of the will of [[Wee Jas]].
	* As such, most people, even those with preferred pronouns, identify as non-binary and are free to express their gender and sexual identities however they like.
	* This is further reinforced by the Crow uniform being highly gender ambiguous (long body concealing robes, large coats that hide the person's frame, face coverings, etc.)

# Conflict
* Roughly 1000 years after its founding, their was a schism in the church of Wee Jas
* The [[Vultura]] believed Wee Jas should be worshipped exclusively in the context of being a death god and that it should be the mission of the church to bring about the death she has fortold.
* the Rest of the Crow's Nest viewed them as radicals obsessed with death and that they deny Wee Jas's power over life as well as death.
* The Vultura left the Crow's Nest and established a base within Burgundy.
* In recent times there have been efforts to mend the gap between the two sects.
	* In the past century, [[Crow Hale]] has been granted Crow status after leaving the [[Vultura]] as a show of good faith.  It did not seem to work, however, and the [[Vultura]] have maintained an arms length distance.

# Clergy Life
* ## Hierarchy of Crows.
	* High Crow.
		* The leader of the Crow's Nest, as distinguished by their feathered mantle.
		* Responsible with approving all missions and issuing all church decrees.
		* The High Crow rarely if ever leaves the Crow's Nest, instead sending out Crows in their stead whenever they have a task that needs accomplishing.
	* Crows.
		* The trained priests of the Crow's Nest (Approximately 5,000).
		* Once you achieve Crowhood, you are allowed to administer healing, exorcism, and examination, vote on important matters and the entry of other crows, issue or raise quarantines on towns and cities, come and go freely from the Crows Nest, and can be assigned missions by the High Crow.
	* Priests in training.
		* Those in the processing of training to become a Crow (approximately 1,000).
		* The test to enter priesthood in the Crows Nest begins when a member reaches their equivalent of adulthood and wishes to enter. The process usually takes around 8-10 years although for some it can take much shorter and for others much longer.
			* It is possible to enter Crowhood without these qualifications, but only if that priest is seen accomplishing a miracle that would be impossible without Wee Jas’s intervention, indicating that they are favored by the Goddess.
	* Followers.
		* Those who live in the Crow's Nest and worship Wee Jas but do not or cannot become a Crow (approximately 10,000).
			* Often the families of Crows and Priests in training.
			* many of the rural worshippers of [[Wee Jas]] only want a place where they can worship their goddess without facing judgment or prosecution.
			* Others are travelling worshippers who come to The Crow's Nest on pilgrimage but do not wish to live their permanently.
			* Still others lack the credentials to become Crows but still worship Wee Jas.  many of them have attempted priesthood but fail for one reason or another.
	* Other.
		* Everyone else who lives in the Crow's Nest but does not worship Wee Jas (approximately 100).
		* They are few in number, mostly locals who got absorbed into the church as it expanded overtime or those seeking shelter from the wilderness.
* ## Council of Crows.
	* The High Crow can summon a Council of Crows for any matter they feel merits a discussion (typically things relating to disease or the presence of undead).
		* Technically, all Crows have this power, but only the High Crow wields the respect and authority to be able to summon next to all Crows together from across the globe.
	* All Crows are allowed a chance to speak at the council, so long as they are in attendance, and a majority decision must be voted on by all Crows in attendance before any action is taken.
	* Every 10 years, a Council of Crows is called to determine the High Crow.
		* A Majority decision must be reached and EVERY Crow must vote, even if they cannot attend.
			* It is highly unusual for a High Crow to not serve for life.
			* [[High Crow Rasster]] has served as High Crow for over 300 years.
		* The non-Crow members of the Crow's Nest also have a say in the proceedings, however their opinion is only meaningful in the event of a hung vote.
		* If a Crow questions the wisdom of the elected High Crow, they can issue a challenge to their rule, forcing an election.
			* For this to occur, the Crow must provide tangible evidence to support their claim.
				* Failure to do so leads to the offending Crow performing a walk of shame around the Crow's Nest on their knees.
			* If the evidence is deemed valid, the High Crow will be given trial where they can be further questioned or plead their case, and afterwards a new election for High Crow is held.
			* Such trials are extremely rare.
				* There has only been one recorded ousting of a High Crow from power in this way, the trial of High Crow Bravo, who was found guilty of Cannibalism.
* ## Daily Life.
	* Daily life in the Crows Nest largely consists of worship, performing rituals, going out to perform last rites, curing sickness, and sanctifying nearby villages and lands.
	* New members of the Crows Nest usually come from families of members, those seeking to convert to worshipping Wee Jas, the destitute with nowhere left to turn in life, and oftentimes those who managed to miraculously recover from an otherwise fatal disease. 
	* Occasionally, new members will join for the opposite reason, because they lost someone and want to know why this had to happen.

# Becoming a Crow
## History
- It is the Crows job to guide people to their correct time of death, and to ease the suffering of those whose time has passed, and extolling judgment on those who are unwilling to part from this mortal coil.
* In the past there were many restrictions on who was and was not allowed to be a Crow.
* The original Crows who served under Eileen were all high level clerics, and thus for nearly a 1000 years only high level clerics could officially hold the title of Crow.
* After the Schism of 5505, the Crow's Nest decided to loosen the restrictions on who was allowed to become a Crow.
	* All priests of [[Wee Jas]] who served at the Crow's Nest prior to 5505 were retroactively dubbed Crows in the process.
## Requirements
* Extensive Medical Knowledge (Expertise in Medicine).
	* In order to train for priesthood, most effectively apply medicine and healing, and be made to confront the realities of life and death, priests in training at The Crows Nest are often exposed to death.
		- They will often have to perform examinations on dead bodies in various states of disease and decay, both young and old.
			- This is the step that most often slows people’s progress, as they struggle to recognize that while death is natural and necessary for the preservation of life, that does not mean it is always easy.
	* Some Knowledge of the Arcane Arts.
		* In modern times, priesthood does not inherently require the ability to perform magic, however it still does require years of rigorous arcane and anatomical study.
	* Knowledge of [[Wee Jas]]'s role in the celestial hierarchy and the history of the Crow's Nest.
		* Priests must learn to memorize all of Wee Jas’s honorifics and alternate names, as well as be able to apply what they’ve learned about the history of the Crows Nest, Wee Jas’s place in the celestial hierarchy, and the effects of medicinal herbs on the fly.
			- The two most common languages that the priests of Wee Jas learn are Celestial and Primordial.  Abyssal is the third most commonly spoken language langauge.
	* Passing the final exam: a written test, autopsy, and application of cultivated skill.
	* And lastly, undergoing the [[Rite of Death]], or Ritual of Death Sight as its sometimes called.
## Class and Look
* While anyone with the proper training CAN become a Crow, most Crows tend to be Clerics, Paladins, and Warlocks.
	* Bards and Druids are also fairly common.
	* Sorcerers, Artificers, Monks, and Wizards are uncommon, but not unheard of.
	* a Ranger, Fighter, or Bloodhunter Crow would be seen as exceptional.
	* Barbarians and rogues are exceedingly rare, almost non-existent, among Crows, but there are a handful.
	* The priests and members of Wee Jas are free to demonstrate their faith to the goddess in whatever method best suits them.
		- So long as it does not contradict the official doctrine regarding the practices of resurrection and life extension.
- Upon achieving Crow status, you are allowed given a plague doctor mask, which you are free to customize however you wish.
	- These masks are all styled after various birds and represent the rank of Crow.
