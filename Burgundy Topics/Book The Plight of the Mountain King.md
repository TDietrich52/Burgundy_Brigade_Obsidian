A book  found by  [[Glim Mekkelferce]] in [[Roi Cercle Library]]. Written from 3663-3669 
It divides into 3 sections

# The Novella 
## Fable Section
-   Fairytales and myths, poems, and lessons to children to avoid the [[The Mountain King]],  Rural Burgundy, and the Grim Mountains. Dated to be written in early 4000s - 4500s


## Fiction Section
### "Oh Cursed is Me"
This was a show preformed by traveling [[Hecklers]] in rural areas in an effort to convince more people to move to the city for safety
#### The Plot 
-   Tone of the show feels as if it was told from someone who was already dead
- A Farmer blames the Mountain king for crop failure and attempts to flee to the city for safety behind it's walls. The farmer also is under the impression his wife left him for another man, possibly the Mountain King
- This section read like an internal dialogue from a play ending with the speaker being cut off


## Fraud Section
The oldest section of this book, prefaced with *“Old record liable to hysteria or misinformation”*
	Tells the tale of Dyre (AKA [[The Mountain King]]), who was banished from Burgundy for practicing forbidden magic in 3663. Following this, [[Hecklers]] began reporting on an attack on a group of Lumberjacks in which only one man survived. He spoke of monsters lead by the Mountain King and wanting to destroy Burgundy. After this the city began to close its walls at night, as they began facing attacks from "mountain monsters", tentacled creatures than would attempt to get into the city 
	
The next section is a Citizens report from Burgundy officials (3668) 
	-   Mountains and farmland uninhabitable due to attacks by the mountain king   
	-   50,000 people died, 100,000 displaced, moved into the city.
	-   BUAT professor added to the notes
	-   Dark Rituals in the mountains.  Unnatural rituals and monsters.
	-   Claimed the Mountain Monsters "appear on short notice, and disappear just as quick"
The last part speaks on a report from BACS (Proto-[[SCAB]])
	-   The mountain king was assassinated in 3669 by a task force. 
	-   Quoted “The Mountain King is Dead.  Rejoice.”
