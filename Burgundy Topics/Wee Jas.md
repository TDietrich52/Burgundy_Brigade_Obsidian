Goddess of Life and Death.
	She is the goddess task with measuring a persons life and determining its start point and end up.
-   Wee Jas, Arbiter of Life and Death.
	-   Wee Jas is viewed as a cyclical goddess with domain over life and death.  It is widely believed that it is her duty to determine a person’s time of death and to maintain their life until they reach that appointed time.
	- Wee Jas also serves as one of the judges of the afterlife, determining what happens to their soul and remains once they have passed on.  It is believed that the followers of Wee Jas are taken in by her when they die, converted into new life, and released back into the mortal planes in order to bring in new life.
	* Wee Jas is as a half living-half skeletal goddess, symbolic of her duality as both a maintainer of life and bringer of death.
		* On her left, Wee Jas is commonly depicted as a beautiful but melancholic woman with brown or black hair, pale icy blue ices, pale green skin, and clothed in robes made of brilliant white light and greenery.
		* On her right, Wee Jas is depicted as a skeleton surrounded by black smoke and blue flames.  
		* In her left hand she is shown holding a sickle, and in her right hand she holds either a handful of seeds or an hourglass, which represents the idea that all life must eventually end and that from death can come new life.

Followers: [[Crow's Nest]], [[Vultura]], [[Glim Mekkelferce]], [[Crow Hale]], [[Crow Rondel]], [[High Crow Rasster]].

# Beliefs
The followers of Wee Jas believe that life is sacred and finite, that all beings must one day die and to deny that would be like denying nature themselves.
While the school of [[Necromancy]] is not strickly forbidden underneath Wee Jas (the power to heal and take life are after all necrotic in nature), the practice of raising the dead, creating undead, artificially extending ones life, and lichdom are seen as heresies of the highest order.
	Exceptions can be made for the victims of vampirism or ghosts that haunt the material plane, but for the perpetrators they are seen as perverting nature itself.
